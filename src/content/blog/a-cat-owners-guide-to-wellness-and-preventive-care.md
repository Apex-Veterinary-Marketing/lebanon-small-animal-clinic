Cats are experts at hiding discomfort, which means health problems often go unnoticed until they become serious. A cat who eats, sleeps, and plays normally may still be developing an issue beneath the surface.

Preventive care helps catch these problems early, even for indoor cats who seem perfectly content. Routine attention at home, paired with regular veterinary visits, keeps your cat healthy and comfortable for years.

This guide covers why cats conceal illness, the core pillars of preventive care, and the early warning signs to watch for at home.


## Why Cats Hide Illness

Cats are both predators and prey by instinct. In the wild, showing weakness makes an animal a target, and masking discomfort is a survival behavior that domestic cats have retained. A sick cat often acts normal until a problem is advanced.

Subtle changes are easy to miss without close attention and regular checkups. This instinct is exactly why preventive care holds such value for cats. Regular exams can reveal what your cat is working hard to conceal.

## What Happens During a Cat Wellness Visit

A wellness visit gives your veterinary team a complete picture of your cat's health. Each visit typically includes:

- **A full physical exam:** Weight, coat, teeth, eyes, ears, heart, and more.
- **Diagnostics:** Bloodwork and other tests that can reveal problems before symptoms appear.
- **Preventive updates:** Vaccinations and parasite prevention reviewed and updated as needed.

These visits also establish a baseline for your cat's normal, making it easier to spot changes over time. Senior cats benefit from twice-yearly visits as age-related conditions become more common.

## 5 Pillars of Preventive Cat Care

Preventive care works best as a consistent, year-round routine. Each of these areas supports your cat's long-term health.

### 1. Vaccinations

Core vaccines protect against serious and common feline diseases. Your veterinarian tailors' recommendations to your cat's lifestyle and risk factors, ensuring the right protection without unnecessary extras.

### 2. Parasite Prevention

Fleas, ticks, and intestinal parasites affect indoor cats too. Parasites can enter your home on clothing, shoes, or other pets, and mosquitoes carrying heartworms find their way inside with ease. Year-round prevention offers the best protection.

### 3. Dental Care

Dental disease is one of the most common health problems in cats, and it often develops quietly. Regular dental checks and at-home care help prevent pain, infection, and tooth loss before they affect your cat's quality of life.

### 4. Nutrition and Weight Management

The right diet supports energy, digestion, and a healthy weight. Obesity is common in cats and increases the risk of diabetes and joint problems. Your veterinary team can recommend portions and food choices suited to your cat's age and activity level.

### 5. Hydration and Kidney Health

Cats are prone to kidney issues, especially as they age. Encouraging water intake through fresh water sources and wet food supports kidney function throughout your cat's life.


## 4 Signs Your Cat May Be Sick

You know your cat best, and small changes often signal something worth checking. Contact your veterinarian if you notice any of the following.

### 1. Changes in Litter Box Habits

Straining, going outside the box, or changes in frequency can indicate urinary or kidney issues. Changes in stools can signal digestive or other health concerns. Litter box habits are one of the most reliable windows into a cat's health.

### 2. Changes in Appetite or Water Intake

Eating less, eating more, or drinking noticeably more water all warrant attention. Increased thirst in particular can be an early sign of kidney disease or diabetes.

### 3. Changes in Grooming or Coat

Overgrooming, under grooming, or a dull, matted coat can point to stress, pain, or illness. A cat who stops grooming may not be feeling well and deserves a closer look.

### 4. Changes in Behavior or Activity

Hiding more, reduced play, irritability, or altered sleep patterns can all signal a problem. Trust your instincts if your cat seems different from their usual self.

## How to Reduce Stress in Cats

Stress affects a cat's physical health in addition to their mood. A calm environment at home and at the clinic supports overall wellbeing.

### Enrichment at Home

Scratching posts, climbing spaces, toys, and daily play sessions keep cats mentally and physically healthy. Enrichment is especially important for indoor cats, who rely on their environment for the stimulation outdoor exploration would otherwise provide.
