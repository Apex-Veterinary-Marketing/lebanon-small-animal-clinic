Holiday meals fill the house with warmth, familiar smells, and hopeful eyes waiting under the table. It’s natural to want to share a taste with your pets, but not every festive dish belongs in their bowl. 

Many pet parents know to avoid chocolate, grapes, and rich desserts, yet questions remain about what’s actually safe to serve. Several holiday foods your pets can enjoy also support their health when prepared simply and served in small portions. 

At Lebanon Small Animal Clinic, our Care Companions help families choose foods that let pets feel included while staying healthy through the holidays. A few small adjustments to your menu can create a celebration that’s joyful and safe for all. 

This article provides you with vet-approved foods along with preparation and portion tips that keep every bite as wholesome as it is festive.

## Festive Foods That Are Pet-Friendly

### 1. Lean Turkey (Plain and Cooked)

Plain, cooked turkey is a healthy source of protein for both dogs and cats. Remove the skin, fat, and bones before serving to prevent stomach upset or choking. Offer only small, bite-sized pieces to keep portions balanced and safe.

### 2. Plain Mashed Potatoes

Potatoes can be a gentle addition to your pet’s holiday plate as long as they are free of butter, garlic, onions, and salt. Keep servings small and unseasoned to avoid digestive upset.

### 3. Steamed Vegetables

Steamed vegetables are one of the easiest ways to share a nutritious side with your pet. Options like carrots, green beans, or peas provide vitamins and fiber that support healthy digestion. Skip the butter, oils, and seasonings to keep them safe.

### 4. Pumpkin (Plain, Cooked, or Canned)

Plain pumpkin makes a tasty and digestion-friendly treat. It’s rich in fiber and nutrients that support gut health. Choose plain cooked or canned pumpkin, not pie filling, which contains added sugar and spices that can upset your pet’s stomach.

### 5. Sweet Potatoes (Plain and Cooked)

Sweet potatoes are a seasonal favorite pets can enjoy too. Serve them plain and cooked, without butter, sugar, or marshmallows. Their natural sweetness and vitamins make them a healthy, festive addition to your pet’s meal.

### 6. Apples (Without Seeds or Core)

Fresh apple slices are a crunchy, refreshing snack for many pets. Remove the seeds and core before serving and offer a few small pieces at a time. Apples provide fiber and vitamins but should be considered an occasional treat rather than a full serving.

### 7. Cranberries (Fresh or Cooked Plain)

Cranberries contain antioxidants that can benefit both dogs and cats. Serve them fresh or cooked without sugar, juice, or added flavors. Avoid cranberry sauces, which are often high in sugar and unsafe ingredients.

### 8. Rice or Plain Pasta

A small serving of plain white rice or pasta can help fill your pet’s belly without irritation. These mild options are gentle on the stomach, making them a smart choice for pets with sensitive digestion. Keep portions small to prevent overfeeding.

### 9. Cheese (Small Amounts)

A small piece of cheese can make a holiday treat for pets who tolerate dairy. Choose low-fat, low-sodium varieties and serve sparingly to avoid stomach upset. Cheese should remain an occasional indulgence rather than a regular snack.

### 10. Pet-Safe Holiday Treats

Store-bought treats formulated for dogs or cats are the safest way to include them in your holiday festivities. Look for vet-approved brands that use simple, high-quality ingredients. These treats satisfy your pet’s curiosity and keep them safely involved in the celebration.
