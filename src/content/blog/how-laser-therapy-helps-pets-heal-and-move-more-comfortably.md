Watching your pet recover slowly from surgery or an injury is hard. It can seem like all you can do is wait and hope things start to improve. There are treatments that can help speed your pet's recovery.

Laser therapy is a drug-free treatment for pain and slow healing. It uses focused light to ease soreness and help your pet's body repair itself. There is no medication involved, no sedation, and most pets settle in quickly once they get used to the routine.

This article takes a closer look at how laser therapy might help your pet.

## How Laser Therapy Uses Light to Reduce Pain and Support Healing

Laser therapy uses a concentrated beam of light that reaches the tissue under your pet's skin. Your pet's cells soak up that light and use the energy to repair themselves. The result is less swelling and faster healing in the sore or injured area.

You might hear your veterinarian call it cold laser therapy or photo biomodulation. Both names describe the same treatment. Nothing about it cuts, burns, or breaks your pet's skin. Your pet stays awake and comfortable the entire time.

## 5 Benefits of Laser Therapy for Dogs and Cats

Laser therapy works gradually. Your pet will likely need a few sessions before you notice a clear change, and how quickly that happens depends on what is being treated.

### 1. Pain Relief Without Medication

Laser therapy calms the pain signals coming from the sore area. This helps when your pet cannot take certain medications, or when their current medication is not doing enough on its own.

### 2. Reduced Inflammation and Swelling

The treatment gets blood moving through the sore area, which helps your pet's body clear away swelling. Less swelling means less pressure on the tissue and nerves nearby, and that brings your pet relief.

### 3. Faster Healing

When cells have more energy, they repair damage faster. This is why laser therapy is often used after surgery, or to help wounds and pulled muscles heal.

### 4. Improved Mobility and Comfort

Pets with sore joints often start moving more freely after a few sessions. Stairs get easier. Play comes back. Senior pets and pets living with arthritis usually see the biggest difference.

### 5. A Low-Stress Experience for Anxious Pets

There are no needles, no sedation, and no recovery time. Most pets find the gentle warmth relaxing and settle right in. If your pet gets nervous at the vet, our Care Companions use Fear-Free handling to keep the whole visit calm.


## Conditions Laser Therapy Commonly Helps Manage

Laser therapy helps with a lot of common problems in dogs and cats:

- Arthritis and other joint problems
- Recovery after surgery, including spay, neuter, and orthopedic procedures
- Wounds, incisions, and skin infections
- Pulled muscles, strains, and sprains
- Ear infections and hot spots
- Back and neck pain
- Long-term conditions in senior pets

## What to Expect During a Laser Therapy Session

The first visit is usually quicker and less involved than owners expect. Here is what happens:

- **Short sessions:** Most treatments take just a few minutes, depending on the area.
- **Protective eyewear:** Your pet wears it, and the team member treating them does too.
- **Gentle handling:** The handpiece moves slowly over the sore spot and gives off a mild warmth.
- **A calm experience:** Most pets settle quickly, and quite a few doze off.
- **No downtime:** Your pet goes home and gets back to normal right away

Pets with long-term conditions usually need a series of sessions. Visits start out closer together, then spread out as your pet improves.

## Is Laser Therapy Right for Your Pet?

Laser therapy works best as one piece of a bigger plan. It pairs well with pain medication, a healthy weight, joint supplements, and regular exercise.

Laser therapy is not the right fit for every pet, including pregnant pets and pets with certain types of tumors. An exam tells us whether it is a safe and helpful choice for your pet.

We also offer holistic treatment options for joint pain that can work alongside laser therapy. If your pet is dealing with pain or healing from a procedure, ask about both at your next visit.
